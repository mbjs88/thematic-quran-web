## Why this surah next

Al-Mulk is the second of the corpus pass and is chosen deliberately to **stress-test different facets than Maryam did**. Maryam was People-heavy (10 prophet labels in use); Al-Mulk is Makki, eschatology-heavy, cosmology-heavy, and largely without named figures — exactly the kind of surah where the taxonomy's middle facets (`divine-attributes`, `eschatology`, `cosmology`, `negative-attributes`) carry the weight. If the labels feel sparse or wrong here, that's a signal that those facets need work before we scale.

It is also one of only four English-audio surahs already provisioned in the project (alongside 2, 78, 79, 114), so users are likely to land here early.

## What this surah revealed about the taxonomy

A few observations worth your call before we scale further:

1. **`prophethood-general` is becoming load-bearing.** I tagged it 3× in Al-Mulk because the "did there not come to you a warner?" pattern is everywhere. Consider whether a more specific label — `warner-pattern` or `messenger-sent-to-people` — would actually help users, or whether `prophethood-general` is fine as the catch-all.
2. **`muhammad` on every "Say:" passage.** Strictly correct under generous tagging, but it will end up on hundreds of sections — almost every Makki surah. The user value is real (filter to "every passage where Muhammad is told to say something") but it's a high-frequency tag that may need a UI demotion. Flag for the filter-UI design.
3. **No `du'a` label hit at all.** Al-Mulk has none, which is fine — it's not a du'a-rich surah. Tested next via Al-Fatihah.
